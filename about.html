Our mission

To share Africa's food heritage through the recipes, ingredients and stories that families have passed down for generations.

A short history

Africa gave the world grains like teff, sorghum and fonio, and coffee first grew in Ethiopia. For centuries, trade across the Sahara and the Indian Ocean carried spices and cooking ideas in and out of the continent.

Later, maize, cassava, tomatoes and chili peppers arrived from the Americas and became everyday staples, the base of dishes like banku and jollof.

Global influence

African cooks carried okra, black-eyed peas and rice-growing knowledge across the Atlantic, shaping cuisines in the Caribbean, Brazil and the American South. Today, African restaurants and spice blends are part of food culture in cities around the world.

Visit a food landmark

Kimironko Market, Kigali. One of Rwanda's busiest markets, where cooks buy fresh cassava leaves, plantains, beans and spices, the everyday ingredients behind dishes like isombe.
